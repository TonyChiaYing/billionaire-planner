# Product behavior reference

## Experience principles

1. Action before administration.
2. Adjustment before abandonment.
3. Outcomes and effort are both visible, never conflated.
4. Rest and uncertainty are planned capacity, not defects.
5. Assistance is explainable and user-confirmed.
6. History survives strategy, schedule, and deadline changes.

## Today screen

Order content as: next action; capacity/load; today's accepted plan grouped by work, personal, rest; changes needing disposition; recent outcome. Limit proactive recovery cards so they do not crowd out action.

Support quick capture with title only. Additional metadata is progressive. A captured item stays inbox/unplanned until explicitly placed or accepted from a draft.

## Focus goals

Recommend three, allow five. On a sixth focus request, offer replace, leave non-focus, or cancel. Do not say the user failed or call goals "energy killers." Non-focus goals remain searchable and editable but do not automatically feed daily drafts.

## Progressive goal setup

Required initial fields: title, horizon, outcome description. Deadline may be absent. Before daily planning, request a current strategy and a small next action. Permit draft save at every step.

## Dual progress

Outcome side: target/value, completed milestones, or qualitative evidence. Action side: completed actions, actual time, planned-versus-done trend. Never calculate a single blended percentage that makes activity look like achieved outcome.

## Planning levels

Default to ordered or broad periods such as morning/afternoon/evening. Timed blocks are optional for users who need precision. Five-minute mode remains an accelerator, not the default or a requirement.

## Work and personal balance

Use one capacity ledger but distinct labels. A work overrun triggers local conflict handling. Protect the user's configured rest floor and any explicitly locked minimum personal-goal action; show tradeoffs if both cannot fit rather than pretending they can.

## Carryover

An unfinished item needs an explicit disposition: continue today, tomorrow, date, waiting, no longer needed, or resize. Keep carryover count and original creation/source, but do not shame repeated carryover. Repetition is a signal for planning or recovery.

## Reviews

Work close is short and work-specific. Evening close confirms the whole day, saves outcomes, and prepares tomorrow. Preselect facts already observed, but require confirmation for inferred actual time or completion. A rest day and "no measurable progress" are valid.

## Recovery

Use language such as "This goal may need a smaller path" rather than "You are behind." Show the evidence behind the prompt. Offer smallest intervention first. A user may dismiss, snooze, pause the goal, or turn off a suggestion class.

Restart creates a new cycle with a visible baseline while accumulated outcomes remain. Deadline changes are append-only. Pausing requires an optional review date; no notifications should fire for ordinary progress while paused.

## Intervention suppression

- Daily: at most one unresolved prompt per issue per planning day.
- Weekly: at most once per seven-day review window.
- Deadline: repeat only when risk changes materially, the deadline window changes, or the user-requested snooze expires.
- Dismissed identical advice receives a cooldown; implementation may centralize the exact duration.

These are baseline throttles, not engagement targets. Never manufacture prompts to increase use.

## Notification privacy

Default lock-screen text is generic. Full titles require explicit preference. Think mode pauses nonessential reminders and restores future schedules on exit without replaying expired notifications.

## Copy guardrails

Avoid: failure, broken streak, lazy, killer, no discipline, overdue as a personal judgment. Prefer: needs review, plan changed, choose a new time, smaller next step, preserve progress, pause intentionally.
